# Fine-tuning LMs for confidence/correctness prediction and OOD transfer (2022-2026), vs. claim C2

Claim C2 (under test): jointly training Qwen2.5-0.5B with LoRA on L_LM + lambda * L_trust (a correctness head on hidden states) does not transfer to a held-out QA dataset better than a frozen-backbone head trained on the same data, nor better than trust-only LoRA. It helps in-distribution only. Every trained head transferred below untrained signals (calibrated raw token confidence, semantic entropy). 2 training sets (TriviaQA, SQuAD) x 3 seeds x 4 arms.

Overall verdict: **(b) partially known.** The general pattern (trained correctness estimators transfer imperfectly to new tasks, and logit baselines are hard to beat) is documented. But the most-cited direct comparison (Kapoor et al. 2024) reports the opposite direction: training through the features (LoRA) beats a frozen probe and generalizes OOD. The specific design (joint L_LM + L_trust vs. frozen head vs. trust-only LoRA vs. LM-then-head, single-source training, held-out QA, compared with calibrated raw confidence and semantic entropy) was not found in prior work. Treat it as an apparently new negative result *for this setup and scale*, not as a new phenomenon.

## Q1. Which papers fine-tune LLMs to predict their own correctness or express calibrated uncertainty, and do they test OOD transfer?

### Takeaway
There is a large body of such work (P(IK), verbalized-confidence tuning, calibration tuning, refusal tuning, RL with Brier rewards, UQ heads). Nearly all report positive OOD or cross-domain transfer, usually after training on many datasets and at 7B+ scale. Kadavath et al. 2022 is the main early exception: transfer is only partial.

### Cited Findings
- **Kapoor et al. 2024, "Large Language Models Must Be Taught to Know What They Don't Know"** (arXiv 2406.08391; arXiv page marks it "NeurIPS 2024 Camera Ready"). It fine-tunes on graded correct/incorrect answers. Quotes: "prompting on its own is insufficient to achieve good calibration"; "training through the features of a model is necessary for good performance"; about a thousand graded examples beat baselines — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08391)
  - Arms: **Probe** (a small MLP on frozen last-layer features), **LoRA** (Probe plus adapters, so features can change), **LoRA + Prompt** (correctness posed as a two-option multiple-choice question), Zero-Shot Classifier (temperature-scaled True/False logits), Verbalized, sampling baselines, and likelihood baselines — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
  - Training data: about 20k examples from 16 benchmarks (ARC, BoolQ, HellaSwag, MathQA, PIQA, Winogrande, ...). Evaluation on MMLU in multiple-choice (MC) and open-ended (OE) form. OOD tests: subject shift, MC-to-OE format transfer, unanswerable questions (SelfAware), cross-model estimation, and LeetCode coding — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
  - Result: "Probe is insufficient to generalize effectively from MC to OE," while LoRA + Prompt "does generalize effectively, even out-performing probe trained on OE data." Frozen features suffice for MC MMLU, but OE needs "training through the model and prompting" — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
  - Models: LLaMA-2 7B/13B and Mistral 7B, plus instruct variants. No sub-1B models — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
  - Baselines: perplexity for OE is "generally not predictive." There is no temperature-scaled token-likelihood baseline for OE, and no semantic-entropy baseline (only counting and likelihood over 10 samples) — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
  - Caveats: the uncertainty estimator is a *separate* fine-tuned copy ("two separate models for question answering and uncertainty estimation"). Single-model uncertainty-aware training is left to future work. On coding with weaker models, "calibration slightly degrades" (LLaMA-2-7B ECE 46.4% for LoRA+Prompt vs. 41.0% for Zero-Shot) — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
- **Kapoor et al. 2024, "Calibration-Tuning: Teaching LLMs to Know What They Don't Know"** (UncertaiNLP workshop 2024, ACL Anthology 2024.uncertainlp-1.1). It fine-tunes the LLM itself to output calibrated probabilities. The abstract claims better calibration on QA, including open-ended, "without reducing accuracy," and says the ability carries over to domains outside the training data — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.uncertainlp-1.1/). A search snippet of the PDF says the authors found that "finetuning the existing model is more effective for calibration than training an auxiliary model on top of frozen features." I could not read the PDF text, so this quote is unverified — [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.uncertainlp-1.1.pdf)
- **Kadavath et al. 2022, "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know"** (arXiv 2207.05221). It trains models to predict P(IK). "Models perform well at predicting P(IK) and partially generalize across tasks," but "they struggle with calibration of P(IK) on new tasks" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)
- **Lin, Hilton, Evans 2022, "Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words"** (arXiv 2205.14334). They fine-tune GPT-3 to verbalize confidence on CalibratedMath, and calibration stays moderately good under distribution shift. Crucially, they compare with logits: "Both kinds of uncertainty are capable of generalizing calibration under distribution shift" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14334). The arXiv page lists no venue. From memory it was published in TMLR 2022 (unverified here).
- **Zhang et al. 2024, R-Tuning** (arXiv 2311.09677; NAACL 2024, ACL Anthology 2024.naacl-long.394). Refusal-aware instruction tuning. The authors report that refusal is a "meta-skill" that generalizes to out-of-domain datasets, and is strengthened by multi-task training — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09677); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.394)
- **Li, Xiong, Wu, Hooi 2025, ConfTuner** (arXiv 2508.18847; NeurIPS 2025 poster). Fine-tunes with a "tokenized Brier score," a proper scoring rule for verbalized confidence. Reports up to 54.7% ECE and 14.4% AUROC improvement over the best baseline, and generalization to black-box models such as GPT-4o. The numbers come from the v1 HTML via a search summary, so check them — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18847v2); [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117676)
- **Damani et al. 2025, RLCR, "Beyond Binary Rewards: Training LMs to Reason About Their Uncertainty"** (arXiv 2507.16806; ICLR 2026 poster). Adds a Brier term to the RLVR reward. Reports improved calibration on out-of-domain tasks, where plain RLVR hurts calibration — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10011036)
- **Ulmer et al. 2024, APRICOT** (arXiv 2403.05973). Trains an *auxiliary* model to predict an LLM's confidence from its input and output text only. Reports calibration error competitive with white-box and black-box baselines on closed-book QA. The abstract states no OOD results — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05973). From memory it was published at ACL 2024 (unverified here).
- **Shelmanov et al. 2025, UQ heads** (arXiv 2505.08200; EMNLP 2025). Supervised heads on attention maps and logits of a frozen LLM. Claims state-of-the-art claim-level hallucination detection in-domain and out-of-domain, but concedes "as with any other supervised method, they work best for 'in-domain' data" — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1809); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08200v1)
- **Liu, Pan, Li, Chen 2024, "Uncertainty Estimation and Quantification for LLMs: A Simple Supervised Approach"** (arXiv 2404.15993). Supervised estimator on hidden activations plus probability features. Reports "robust transferability in out-of-distribution settings" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15993v4)
- **GrACE** (arXiv 2509.09438). Trains confidence with a regression loss plus an SFT term kept as a regularizer, because fine-tuning "towards only the calibration objective will sacrifice the model's overall performance." This is structurally the closest to the L_LM + lambda * L_trust joint objective that I found — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09438). Search snippet only; OOD details unverified.

### Inferences
- The positive-transfer literature differs from the C2 setup on several axes, any of which could explain the opposite outcome: (i) multi-dataset training sets (Kapoor: 16 benchmarks; R-Tuning finds multi-task training strengthens transfer) vs. single-source training on TriviaQA or SQuAD; (ii) 7B+ backbones vs. 0.5B; (iii) baselines that omit calibrated raw token confidence on open-ended QA and omit semantic entropy (Kapoor's OE likelihood baseline is uncalibrated perplexity).
- So C2 does not directly contradict Kapoor et al. It is a narrower test, at small scale and single-source, against stronger untrained baselines. The write-up should present it as such and should cite Kapoor et al. as the main counterpoint.
- Lin et al. 2022 already found that logit-based uncertainty generalizes under shift about as well as trained verbalized uncertainty. This supports the C2 sub-finding that untrained calibrated raw confidence is a strong transfer baseline.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Calibration-Tuning PDF, so the quote "fine-tuning beats an auxiliary model on frozen features" and its OOD details are unverified.
- I did not verify that Kapoor et al. report a *plain* LoRA (no prompt) vs. Probe comparison under OOD. The page summary says most Probe comparisons are against LoRA + Prompt.
- I did not check Band et al. 2024 (Linguistic calibration), Mielke et al. 2022, Tian et al. 2023 ("Just ask for calibration"), Yang et al. "Alignment for Honesty," or Feng et al. 2024 ("Don't hallucinate, abstain") in this pass. From memory these are mostly verbalized- or prompt-based, or fine-tuning without a frozen-vs-joint comparison, but this is unverified.

## Q2. Is there prior work comparing joint (LM + trust loss) vs. frozen-backbone training of correctness heads, specifically for OOD transfer?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that runs the four-arm comparison (frozen head, trust-only LoRA, joint LM+trust LoRA, LM-only LoRA then head) and tests transfer to a held-out QA dataset. The closest prior work is Kapoor et al. (frozen Probe vs. LoRA/LoRA+Prompt, favoring fine-tuning). The classic linear-probe vs. fine-tuning OOD result (Kumar et al. 2022) is a conceptual precedent that favors frozen features under shift.

### Cited Findings
- Kapoor et al. 2024: training through features beats a frozen Probe, both for MC-to-OE generalization and for OE in general. Its estimator is a separate model and has no L_LM term — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3)
- Kumar et al. 2022, "Fine-Tuning can Distort Pretrained Features and Underperform Out-of-Distribution" (arXiv 2202.10054; ICLR 2022, venue from memory). Fine-tuning can be worse OOD than linear probing when features are good and the shift is large. Per a search summary, linear probing is better on 8 of 10 OOD datasets. This is a vision/general setting, not LLM correctness — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.10054)
- "Preserving Pre-trained Features Helps Calibrate Fine-tuned Language Models" (arXiv 2305.19249). A joint auxiliary MLM loss during fine-tuning lowers ECE (BERT-era NLU, not LLM correctness heads). If the MLM weight is too large, the task is damaged — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.19249)
- GrACE (arXiv 2509.09438) uses the joint calibration + SFT loss as a regularizer. No frozen-vs-joint transfer comparison was seen in the snippet — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.09438)

### Inferences
- The "joint L_LM + L_trust vs. frozen vs. trust-only vs. LM-then-head" ablation appears to be new as a controlled, preregistered comparison. The finding that joint training only helps in-distribution is consistent with the feature-distortion account (Kumar 2022). It runs against the "train through the features" message of Kapoor 2024.
- The novelty is the controlled null and negative result, not a new mechanism.

### Gaps
- My search was limited (about 15 tool calls). A paper with a multitask "uncertainty-aware fine-tuning" ablation could exist that I missed, so "no prior found" is not proof of absence.

## Q3. Prior negative results: do trained correctness predictors fail to transfer, and do they beat calibrated logits or semantic entropy OOD?

### Takeaway
Negative or limited-transfer results exist, mostly for **frozen hidden-state probes** (Orgad et al. 2024; Ha & Vo 2025; Kadavath 2022 partial generalization). Few papers report that *fine-tuned* (LoRA or joint) estimators fall below untrained calibrated baselines OOD. That part of C2 is the least-precedented.

### Cited Findings
- **Orgad et al. 2024, "LLMs Know More Than They Show"** (arXiv 2410.02707; ICLR 2025, venue from memory). Probing classifiers for error detection "do not generalize across different tasks." Generalization happens only within tasks needing similar skills, such as factual retrieval. A follow-up analysis notes most datasets show little or no meaningful generalization once the logit-based baseline is subtracted. That last claim comes from a search summary of a secondary source, so it is unverified — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02707)
- **Ha & Vo 2025** (arXiv 2507.03998, preprint). Hidden-state probes for correctness "often struggle to generalise to another dataset of a different task or domain." Adding data-agnostic features helps only inconsistently — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03998)
- **Kadavath et al. 2022**: P(IK) "partially generalize[s] across tasks" but struggles "with calibration of P(IK) on new tasks" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)
- **Mahaut et al. 2024, "Factual Confidence of LLMs"** (arXiv 2406.13415; ACL 2024 per the arXiv comment). Trained probes on hidden states were the most reliable estimators *in their evaluation*, and confidence is "often unstable across semantically equivalent inputs." The abstract reports no cross-dataset generalization — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13415)
- **Lin et al. 2022**: logit-based and trained verbalized uncertainty both generalize under shift, so no clear win for training — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14334)
- **Shelmanov et al. 2025**: supervised UQ heads "work best for 'in-domain' data" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08200v1)
- Contrasting positive claims: Liu et al. 2024 ("robust transferability" OOD) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15993v4); Kapoor et al. 2024 (LoRA+Prompt generalizes MC to OE) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.08391v3); RLCR (improves OOD calibration) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16806v2)

### Inferences
- "Frozen correctness probes transfer poorly across datasets" is **established** (Orgad 2024, Ha & Vo 2025, Kadavath 2022).
- "Fine-tuning the backbone (alone or jointly with the LM loss) does not fix cross-dataset transfer, and all trained heads fall below calibrated raw token confidence and semantic entropy on held-out QA" is **partially known at most**. It conflicts with the headline claims of Kapoor 2024, Calibration-Tuning, R-Tuning, and RLCR. Those papers mostly train on many datasets, use larger models, and omit calibrated-logit and semantic-entropy OE baselines. A careful write-up should frame C2 as: "in a single-source, small-model setting, with strong untrained baselines, the transfer gains reported for fine-tuned estimators do not appear."
- Recommended citations for the paper: Kapoor et al. 2024 (main counterpoint), Kadavath 2022, Orgad 2024, Lin 2022, Kumar 2022 (mechanism), and Shelmanov 2025 / Ha & Vo 2025 (in-domain limitation).

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether any paper reports semantic entropy (Farquhar et al. 2024, Nature) beating trained correctness heads on held-out QA. Kossen et al. 2024 "Semantic Entropy Probes" (arXiv 2406.15927, from memory) is likely relevant but was not checked here.
- Exact OOD numbers for Liu et al. 2024, Calibration-Tuning, and ConfTuner were not extracted.
- Venues from memory and not verified in this pass: Lin 2022 (TMLR), APRICOT (ACL 2024), Orgad (ICLR 2025), Kumar (ICLR 2022).
