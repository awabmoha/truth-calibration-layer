# Grounding detection from internals, irrelevant context and abstention, and NLI grounding-label reliability (prior work 2018-2026)

Scope: novelty check for C4 (one-pass hidden-state grounding head, distractor passages, scale-dependent abstention) and C5 (DeBERTa-MNLI grounding label: human agreement 76-79%, template flips a preregistered conclusion).

Verification legend: [V] = arXiv ID / venue confirmed in this session from arXiv, ACL Anthology, ICLR proceedings or publisher page. [T] = from my prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session; check before citing. [P] = 2026 preprint seen only as a search snippet; content not read, treat as provisional.

## 1. Detecting unsupported answers in context-based QA/RAG from model internals

### Takeaway
Detecting contextual (RAG) hallucination from internals is a crowded, established area (Lookback Lens, ReDeEP, probes on RAGTruth, HalluRAG, PRISM), and so is the finding that such detectors transfer poorly across tasks/datasets. The specific C4 decomposition (the head's gain over raw confidence comes almost entirely from flagging answers given from an irrelevant passage, and adds little among right-passage answers) is not stated as a result in any paper I found; it is close in spirit to Dubanowska et al.'s "spurious correlation" critique and to GRADE's note that hidden states shift with context presence, so it is best framed as partially known / a sharpening, not a new phenomenon.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Lookback Lens (Chuang, Qiu, Hsieh, Krishna, Kim, Glass; EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2407.07071): a linear classifier on per-head ratios of attention to context vs. generated tokens detects contextual hallucinations about as well as detectors using full hidden states or a text-based entailment model; abstract says it "is found to transfer across tasks and even models" (7B detector applied to 13B without retraining). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07071)
- [V] ReDeEP (Sun et al.; ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.11414): attributes RAG hallucination to copying heads under-using external context and FFNs over-injecting parametric knowledge; regresses an external-context score and a parametric-knowledge score; uses SEP linear probes on hidden states as a baseline. — [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/7daf60e805e596c3bd1e843e72ea5560-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11414)
- [V] Dubanowska, Żelaszczyk, Brzozowski, Mandica, Karpowicz, "Representation-based Broad Hallucination Detectors Fail to Generalize Out of Distribution" (Findings of EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2509.19372): SOTA performance on RAGTruth is "largely driven by a spurious correlation with data"; after controlling for it, SOTA methods do no better than supervised linear probes; out-of-distribution, all analysed methods perform "close to random". The abstract does not name the spurious cue. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19372); [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.952.pdf)
- [V] CH-Wang, Van Durme, Eisner, Kedzie, "Do Androids Know They're Only Dreaming of Electric Sheep?" (Findings of ACL 2024, arXiv 2312.17249): probes for grounded-generation hallucination are "sensitive to their training domain", transfer poorly between tasks and from synthetic to organic hallucinations; in-domain they reach 95% of peak performance by layer 4. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17249)
- [V] PRISM, Zhang et al., "Prompt-Guided Internal States for Hallucination Detection of LLMs" (ACL 2025, arXiv 2411.04847): supervised detectors trained in one domain often fail in others because internal states carry much domain-specific, truth-irrelevant information; prompts are used to make truthfulness structure more consistent across domains. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1058); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04847)
- [V, snippet] HalluRAG (arXiv 2412.17056): MLPs on internal states trained on HalluRAG and tested on RAGTruth performed worse than in-domain; authors suggest general overfitting. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.17056)
- [P] GRADE (arXiv 2604.02830): hidden states shift with the mere presence of retrieved documents regardless of answer correctness, making hidden-state knowledge-gap detectors vulnerable to irrelevant input variation. This is the closest statement I found to "probes may track context presence/relevance rather than support". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02830v2)
- [P] Knowledge-attribution probing (arXiv 2602.22787): whether a model relies on the given context is linearly decodable from mid-to-upper layers; test setup pairs questions with random irrelevant paragraphs. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22787v2)
- [V, snippet] Probing-RAG (Findings of NAACL 2025): a prober on intermediate hidden states decides whether retrieval is needed (not whether a passage supports the answer). — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.181.pdf)
- [V, snippet] CLEAR (arXiv 2510.12460): MLP probe on hidden states flags context/parametric knowledge conflict; prunes irrelevant sentences first. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12460v1)
- [P] Grounding Probes (arXiv 2610.04642): linear probe on an observer model's hidden states reaches 0.879-0.894 AUROC on RAGTruth test; transfer loses 0.10-0.17 AUROC on some splits. [P] A token-level RAGTruth paper (arXiv 2608.18115) reports generator hidden-state linear probes at only 0.543-0.574 AUC vs ReDeEP 0.733 and Lookback Lens 0.73. These two conflict on how well probes work on RAGTruth; both unread beyond snippets. — [2610.04642](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04642); [2608.18115](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18115)
- [T] RAGTruth (Niu et al., ACL 2024, arXiv 2401.00396): word-level annotated RAG hallucination corpus (QA, data-to-text, summarization) used to train/evaluate most of the detectors above. [T] SAPLMA (Azaria & Mitchell, Findings of EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2304.13734): hidden-state truthfulness classifier (closed-book statements, not RAG). [T] LLM-Check (Sriramanan et al., NeurIPS 2024): single-response detection from hidden states/attention/logits, includes RAG settings. Not re-verified this session.

### Inferences
- C4's ingredients (one-pass head on hidden states, comparison against confidence, held-out-dataset evaluation, poor cross-task transfer) are all established. Held-out-dataset evaluation on MRQA (including RelationExtraction) is a reasonable design but not a novelty.
- The decomposition that the head's added value is concentrated in irrelevant-passage cases is consistent with, and a concrete instance of, the "spurious correlation"/domain-information critiques (Dubanowska et al.; PRISM; GRADE). I found no paper that explicitly stratifies a grounding probe's gain by relevant-vs-distractor passage and reports near-zero gain within relevant passages. Verdict: (b) partially known; the stratified result itself appears new but is a modest, expected-looking sharpening.
- Because the distractors in C4 are artificially inserted unrelated passages, reviewers will likely say the head learns a relevance (topic-match) detector, which existing work on context-reliance probing already shows is linearly decodable. The claim should be framed as a negative/diagnostic finding, not as a method contribution.

### Gaps
- I could not read Dubanowska et al.'s full text to see which spurious cue they identify (task type? response length? context?); if it is context relevance, C4 overlaps more strongly.
- Did not verify LLM-Check, SAPLMA, RAGTruth details this session.
- No paper found that tests the same probe across 0.5B/3.8B/7B models where the larger model's abstention removes the detectable case.

## 2. LLM behavior with irrelevant/distractor passages and abstention, and scale

### Takeaway
That irrelevant passages hurt or change LLM QA behavior, that NLI filtering of non-entailing passages is a known baseline, and that random passages behave very differently from related distractors are all established. Evidence that abstention improves with scale is mixed: AbstentionBench finds little scale effect overall, while Sufficient Context finds strong models still answer instead of abstaining. C4's observation that Qwen2.5-7B abstains on 1,996/2,000 clearly unrelated passages (vs. many answers from 0.5B and Phi-3.5-mini) is unsurprising for random/unrelated passages (Wen et al. 2024 find random context makes abstention easier), so it is partially known; its consequence for evaluating detectors (the easy case the head detected disappears at scale) is a reasonable but minor new point.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Yoran, Wolfson, Ram, Berant, "Making Retrieval-Augmented LMs Robust to Irrelevant Context" (arXiv 2310.01558; ICLR 2024 per ICLR virtual site and mlanthology; one iclr.cc listing also places it in an ICLR 2024 workshop, venue listing inconsistent): irrelevant retrieved evidence hurts accuracy; a baseline uses an NLI model to filter passages that do not entail the question-answer pair, which prevents drops but also discards relevant passages; fine-tuning on ~1,000 mixed relevant/irrelevant examples gives robustness. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01558); [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/18397); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2024/yoran2024iclr-making)
- [V] Cuconasu et al., "The Power of Noise" (SIGIR 2024, arXiv 2401.14887): distinguishes relevant, distracting (related but answer-free) and random passages; adding random documents improved accuracy by up to 35%, while high-ranked distracting documents hurt. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14887)
- [P] "The Powerless Noise" (arXiv 2607.03615) reproduces the result but argues the effect stems from prompt format, decoding constraints and lenient evaluation and does not persist under modern RAG practice. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03615)
- [V] Joren et al., "Sufficient Context: A New Lens on RAG Systems" (ICLR 2025, arXiv 2411.06037): autorater classifies sufficient context (93% accuracy); SOTA LLMs output correct responses 35-62% of the time with insufficient context; proprietary models often answer incorrectly instead of abstaining when context is insufficient; selective generation using sufficient-context signals improves accuracy-when-answering by 2-10% (Gemini, GPT, Gemma). — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/33dffa2e3d2ab74a783d1a8c292f66d9-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037v3)
- [V] Wen, Howe, Wang, "Characterizing LLM Abstention Behavior in Science QA with Context Perturbations" (Findings of EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2404.12452): replacing gold context with irrelevant context, or adding irrelevant context, can improve abstention (and accuracy), described as counterintuitive; many LLMs fail to abstain on boolean questions; no model abstains consistently in all expected settings. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12452)
- [V] AbstentionBench (arXiv 2506.09038): scale has almost no effect on abstention (Llama 3.1 8B/70B/405B, Llama 3.3 70B); frontier models close to Qwen2.5-32B and Llama-3.1-8B. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09038)
- [V] Wen et al., "Know Your Limits: A Survey of Abstention in LLMs" (TACL 2025, vol. 13 pp. 529-556, arXiv 2407.18418): organizes abstention by query, model and human-values perspectives; reviews methods, benchmarks, metrics. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18418)
- [V, snippet] Feng et al., "Don't Hallucinate, Abstain" (ACL 2024): includes linear-probe abstention baselines on question encodings; retrieved context weakens some calibration-based abstention methods. — [summary page](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/acl__2024__2024.acl-long.786)
- [P] Small frozen RAG models 3.8B-8B (arXiv 2608.22228): answer rate at or below 3% when the answer is missing, rising to 13.6-74.3% when a fluent passage supports a wrong answer. [P] Another study (arXiv 2609.37469) reports 12 generators 135M-32B still answering roughly 40-99% of insufficient-evidence questions when told to abstain. Both unread beyond snippets. — [2608.22228](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22228); [2609.37469](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.37469)
- [T] Shi et al., "LLMs Can Be Easily Distracted by Irrelevant Context" (ICML 2023, arXiv 2302.00093, GSM-IC); Kadavath et al., "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" (arXiv 2207.05221; calibration/P(IK) improves with scale); Rajpurkar et al., SQuAD 2.0 "Know What You Don't Know" (ACL 2018). Not re-verified this session.

### Inferences
- Unrelated (random) distractors are the easiest abstention case; related-but-answer-free distractors (Cuconasu's "distracting", Joren's "insufficient context") are the hard case. C4's distractors are unrelated, so near-total abstention at 7B under an explicit "unanswerable" instruction is expected rather than novel. Verdict on the scale/abstention part: (a)/(b) largely known.
- The novel-looking element is methodological: a detector's measured advantage can be an artifact of a smaller model's failure to abstain, and disappears once the base model handles the easy case. I did not find this stated for grounding probes, but it is analogous to general "evaluation artifact" critiques (Dubanowska et al.; Powerless Noise).

### Gaps
- No paper found that tracks a grounding detector's gain across model scale while the base model's abstention rate changes.
- Did not find Qwen2.5-specific abstention-with-irrelevant-context numbers.

## 3. Reliability of NLI-based grounding/faithfulness labels for QA answers

### Takeaway
Converting QA to NLI (QA2D, Chen et al. 2021) and using NLI for faithfulness (TRUE, SummaC, AlignScore, MiniCheck) are established, as is that hypothesis wording and conversion steps materially change NLI outputs and that NLI verifiers produce false negatives on correct answers. C5's specific numbers (76-79% agreement with human audit; errors mostly marking supported short answers unsupported) are a new measurement on this setup but consistent with known failure modes; the demonstration that a hypothesis-template change flips a preregistered conclusion (2/7 to 5/7 pairs) is the most distinctive part, and I found no prior paper showing a label-construction choice flipping a preregistered hypothesis test. Verdict: (b) partially known, with the "flips a preregistered conclusion" framing apparently new as a case study.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Demszky, Guu, Liang, "Transforming QA Datasets Into NLI Datasets" (QA2D, arXiv 1809.02922): if A is correct, (passage, declarative of Q+A) is an entailed pair; if A is wrong or Q unanswerable from P, it is not entailed. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.02922)
- [V] Chen, Choi, Durrett, "Can NLI Models Verify QA Systems' Predictions?" (Findings of EMNLP 2021, arXiv 2104.08731): premise = answer sentence (decontextualized), hypothesis = question converted with answer by a T5-3B converter; both converter and decontextualizer are "critical" ("removing either module harms the performance for all datasets"); selective QA F1 81.6 to 87.1 at 20% coverage; QA datasets encourage answering when context is insufficient. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08731); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.324.pdf)
- [V] Chen et al. error analysis (Table 1, NQ-NLI+MNLI verifier) of false negatives on correct answers: NQ 77 FN (4 pipeline, 34 premise, 39 entailment model); TriviaQA 74 (2, 58, 14); SQuAD2.0 69 (7, 6, 56). Pipeline (conversion + decontextualization) errors are under 10%; most false negatives come from premise problems or the NLI model itself. Base model is an off-the-shelf RoBERTa MNLI model. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.08731)
- [V] Adlakha, BehnamGhader, Lu, Meade, Reddy, "Evaluating Correctness and Faithfulness of Instruction-Following Models for QA" (TACL 2024, arXiv 2307.16877): human evaluation reveals shortcomings of traditional correctness and faithfulness metrics; models often fail to stay within provided knowledge. (Abstract does not give NLI-vs-human numbers.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.16877); [TACL](https://direct.mit.edu/tacl/article/doi/10.1162/tacl_a_00667/121196/Evaluating-Correctness-and-Faithfulness-of)
- [V, snippet] Hypothesis wording sensitivity: hypothesis construction "plays a crucial role" in NLI zero-shot performance with model-specific effects (arXiv 2409.13735); meaning-preserving hypothesis paraphrases yield sizable inconsistent NLI decisions (arXiv 2401.14440); Ma et al. (ACL 2021 short) report issues with entailment-based zero-shot classification. — [2409.13735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13735); [2401.14440](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.14440v1); [Ma et al.](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2021.acl-short.99.pdf)
- [P] "The Labeling Problem in Hallucination Detection Benchmarks" (arXiv 2610.08026): strict reference-entailment NLI labels are false-positive-dominant (FP 98.3-99.4%) against a factual-correctness benchmark because NLI penalizes true details absent from short references. Note the direction here is the reverse configuration (answer vs short reference), so not directly comparable to C5. [P] A lightweight-detector benchmark (arXiv 2606.29809) reports short entity-only QA answers are a main error source because they give the NLI model too little to confirm entailment. — [2610.08026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.08026); [2606.29809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29809)
- [V] Laurer, van Atteveldt, Casas, Welbers, "Less Annotating, More Classifying" (Political Analysis 32(1):84-100, 2024; DOI 10.1017/pan.2023.20; online June 2023): BERT-NLI fine-tuning beats classical models by 10.7-18.3 pp on eight tasks. This is the methodological source for the MoritzLaurer DeBERTa-v3 NLI models; the WANLI-trained checkpoint itself is documented on Hugging Face model cards rather than in this paper (not verified in-paper). — [Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/S1047198723000207); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:polals:v:32:y:2024:i:1:p:84-100_6)
- [T] TRUE (Honovich et al., NAACL 2022, arXiv 2204.04991): large NLI (T5-11B on ANLI) and QG-QA are strongest factual-consistency metrics across 11 datasets. [T] SummaC (Laban et al., TACL 2022): sentence-level NLI aggregation fixes granularity mismatch. [T] AlignScore (Zha et al., ACL 2023, arXiv 2305.16739): unified alignment function trained on many tasks including QA. [T] MiniCheck (Tang, Laban, Durrett, EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2404.10774): small grounding fact-checkers matching GPT-4. [T] FactCC (Kryściński et al., EMNLP 2020). Not re-verified this session; none, to my knowledge, report a human-audit agreement for DeBERTa-large-MNLI on short extractive answers with "question answer" hypotheses.

### Inferences
- That a naive "question answer" concatenation hypothesis is worse than a declarative/template hypothesis is exactly what QA2D and Chen et al. motivate; C5's finding that the template changes labels is expected. What is less documented is the downstream consequence: a labeling choice changing the pass/fail outcome of a preregistered hypothesis. This is a useful robustness/reporting point (and the ungated re-analysis is properly labeled), but it should be framed as a cautionary case study, not a new phenomenon.
- C5's error direction (supported short answers called unsupported) partially matches the short-answer fragility noted in 2606.29809 [P], and contrasts with Chen et al., where pipeline-conversion false negatives were under 10% and most false negatives came from premise insufficiency or the NLI model itself. Since C5 uses the full passage as premise (Chen et al. used a decontextualized sentence), more NLI-model-side false negatives on long premises is plausible and consistent with SummaC's granularity argument [T].
- 76-79% agreement is in a range that would surprise no one in this literature; I found no directly comparable published number for DeBERTa-large-MNLI on MRQA-style answers, so the exact figure is new but not a contribution by itself.

### Gaps
- Could not read numeric converter-vs-raw-question comparisons in Chen et al. (Figure 5 values not in text).
- Did not find a paper reporting human agreement for MNLI-based grounding labels on extractive QA answers specifically.
- TRUE, SummaC, AlignScore, MiniCheck, FactCC, Shi et al., Kadavath et al., RAGTruth, SAPLMA, LLM-Check details are from prior knowledge and were not re-fetched; verify IDs/venues before citing.
- Several 2026 arXiv items (2602.x-2610.x) were seen only as search snippets; verify existence and content before citing.
